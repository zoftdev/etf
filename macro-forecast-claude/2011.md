# Macro forecast & outlook (views from 2011 → 2012, 2013 & beyond)

Forecasts and outlooks **published in 2011** that referred to 2012, 2013 or the following years. Used to extract: top 5 countries, top commodity, US sector.

**Scoring (-1–1):** < 0.3 = not much — remove or set low; use score to prioritise. No clear "hot" commodity → low score or omit.

---

## 1. Top 5 countries (2011 view for 2012/2013+)

From 2011-era reports (IMF WEO Jan Update & Apr 2011, World Bank GEP Jan 2011 "Navigating Strong Currents", Goldman Sachs N-11/MIST launch):

| Rank | Country       | Score | Source / note |
|------|---------------|-------|---------------|
| 1    | **China**     | 0.95  | IMF Jan 2011 projected 9.6% (2011) and 9.5% (2012); "two-speed recovery" leader; Goldman Sachs/PwC still saw China overtaking US by mid-2020s. |
| 2    | **India**     | 0.90  | IMF Jan 2011 projected 8.4% (2011) and 8.0% (2012); "Developing Asia continues to grow most rapidly"; BRIC. |
| 3    | **Indonesia** | 0.80  | MIST/N-11 standout; Goldman Sachs launched N-11 Equity Fund (Feb 2011) with Indonesia as a top holding; ~6.2% growth projected; each MIST nation >1% of global GDP. |
| 4    | **Brazil**    | 0.65  | BRIC; IMF projected 4.5% (2011), 4.1% (2012); slowing from 7.5% in 2010 but still among largest emerging economies. |
| 5    | **Russia**    | 0.55  | BRIC; IMF projected 4.5% (2011), 4.4% (2012); moderate recovery; still in long-term top-economies narrative. |

*Alternative 5th from MIST: **South Korea** (highest Growth Environment Score among N-11), **Turkey** (~6–8% growth in 2010–11), or **Mexico** (MIST; >1% of global GDP).*

## 2. Top commodity (2011 view for 2012/2013+)

From **IMF WEO Apr 2011** ("Tensions from the Two-Speed Recovery: Unemployment, **Commodities**, and Capital Flows"), CNNMoney commodity outlook (Jan 2011), PwC Canadian Mining survey:

| Commodity              | Score | Note |
|------------------------|-------|------|
| **Crude oil / energy** | 0.85  | Peaked at $101.80/bbl in Jan 2011; commodity supercycle at its height; IMF Apr 2011 dedicated chapter to commodity/capital-flow tensions; strategists forecast $100+ sustained. |
| **Gold**               | 0.80  | PwC mining survey forecast $1,500/oz for 2011; CNNMoney experts called $1,500–$1,800; actual peak $1,920 in Sep 2011; clearly "hot". |
| Silver                 | 0.50  | Speculative surge to $47.94 in Apr 2011; high volatility (−12% in a single day in May); secondary to gold/oil. |

*Commodity supercycle peaked in 2011. "Driven by strong demand from emerging nations and low supply growth" (Wikipedia, 2000s commodities boom).*
